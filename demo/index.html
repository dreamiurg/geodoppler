Acoustic geolocation · ADS-B flight history

Where was this recorded? Listen for the planes.

A small plane passing overhead makes a tone that slides down as it goes by, the same effect as a passing siren. The shape of that slide tells you how far away the plane was, how fast it flew, and the exact second it passed. Pinpoint finds these slides in a video's sound and matches them against public flight-tracking history. Only one spot on the ground would hear those real planes at those exact moments.

It reads no title, description, tags, GPS, or video frames. It gets the audio, the day, and a wide search area of up to 300 km around the region the uploader names.

How we know it is right
Check 1: the place

The answer has to land where the uploader says the camera is, for example "Northeast Ohio". The tool searched a circle of about 70,000 km² and never saw the description.

Check 2: the clock

The tool also works out when the recording started, to the second. For livestream archives, YouTube records the real go-live time, which the tool never sees. A lucky wrong guess would get this wrong by minutes or hours.

The honest numbers

  • About 200 YouTube results for backyard and home-cam searches. Most were AI-generated music and ambience loops.
  • 52 real US outdoor recordings screened. 36 had at least two possible flyovers, and 20 got a full blind search.
  • 4 recordings passed both checks: 3 days from the Ohio feeder and 1 from Kentucky. Two earlier validation runs also pass: a Huron, Ohio train cam (0.26 km from the camera, start 33 s off, searched 300 km wide) and colsto's recording, the video that started the idea.

Why most videos don't work

  • No planes. Most recordings never hear a small plane within about 8 km during a two-hour window.
  • Jets. Under an airline approach (Northern Kentucky near CVG), the clean tones come from jets. The tool skips jets, because dozens of them fly the same path.
  • Edits and loops. A "1 hour" Boston backyard recording turned out to be a 30-minute recording played twice.
  • Unknown day. Home videos rarely say which day they were filmed, and the upload date is often a different day.
